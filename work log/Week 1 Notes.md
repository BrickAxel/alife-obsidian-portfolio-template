1. Relevance: whether the model tests and generates hypotheses applicable to biology.
Biological relevance: Is the biological target system clearly identified? Does the model generate hypotheses for biology?
2. Generality: the range of biological systems the model can represent.
Generality: How many systems does the model target?
3. Abstraction: the complexity, relative to the target, or amount of detail included in the model.
Abstraction: How many elements and processes from the target are included in the model?
4. Medium: the physical basis by which the model is implemented.
Medium: What is the simulation built from?
5. Level: the elemental units of the model in the hierarchy from atoms to societies.
Level: What are the base units of the model?
6. Performance match: to what extent the model behavior matches the target behavior.
Match: To what extent does the model behave like the target?
7. Structural accuracy: how well the model represents the actual mechanisms underlying the behavior.
Structural accuracy: Is the model a true representation of the target?
